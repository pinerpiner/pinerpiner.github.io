Lü Pin 吕频

I am a Ph.D. candidate in political science at Rutgers University, with the degree expected in summer 2027. My work is situated at the intersection of authoritarian politics, gender and politics, and social movement studies, with China as its empirical focus.

My dissertation advances compensatory adaptation as a framework for analyzing the trajectory of the Chinese feminist movement between 2013 and 2023. I argue that escalating repression redirected movement activity rather than simply reducing it, so that the movement expanded its social reach as its capacity for direct political challenge contracted, and persisted as a form of contentious discourse.

A second line of research theorizes the suppression matrix, a configuration of coercive practices that acts upon feminist activists largely outside the criminal law. I show how the matrix forecloses their possibilities for action and, in doing so, drives the decline of the movement.

My research has been supported by the Carrie Chapman Catt Prize for Research on Women and Politics and by the Women, Gender, and Politics Research Section of the American Political Science Association. My findings have appeared or are forthcoming in China Perspectives and Politics & Gender.

Outside the academy, I have worked in China’s feminist movement for close to thirty years.
