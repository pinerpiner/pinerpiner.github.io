Dissertation

Resistant Resilience in the Age of Escalating Repression: Understanding the Dynamics of China’s Feminist Movement, 2013–2023

This dissertation asks why China's feminist movement remained active under the intensifying repression of the party-state between 2013 and 2023, when many other movements withdrew from public view. It locates the explanation primarily in the iterated interaction between state and movement, in which movement actors exercised agency under continuous constraint. Building on scholarship on the repression-mobilization nexus, it develops the concept of reconfigurative adaptation, which describes how a movement adjusts the configuration of its strategy to evade repression and preserve its vitality. Reconfiguration is measured along two dimensions: politicization, the contentiousness that marks movement activity to the state as a challenge to authoritarian order, and popularization, the social base that makes suppression unattractive to the state.

Coding these dimensions in an original database of more than 500 feminist mobilization events, the dissertation traces the movement's trajectory across the crackdowns of 2015, 2018, and 2021. More than 50 interviews with activists and an extensive body of movement archives reconstruct how dispersed actors read their environment, weighed their options, and arrived at the shifts the database records. With each crackdown, the movement scaled back politicization while expanding popularization, moving from online mobilization for institutional accountability, to dispersed cultural and social critique, and finally to discourse circulating in popular culture. What emerged was an antagonistic coexistence, in which the state ceased to treat the movement as an uncontainable threat and the movement persisted as a vehicle for dissenting discourse and culture.

The dissertation reconceptualizes strategic adaptation as reconfiguration unfolding across distinct dimensions, and its coding scheme makes movement change traceable by integrating individual actions into a temporal sequence. It also offers a close case of state and movement in interaction, revising how the resilience of each is understood.

Journal articles

  • Lü, Pin. Forthcoming. “The Repression Matrix: Non-Spectacular Coercion and Feminist Demobilization in China.” China Perspectives. Manuscript

    Conceptualizes a repression matrix in which the police and state security agencies orchestrate coercion that actors with power over activists’ everyday lives carry out in dispersed and continuous fashion, exhausting their will and resources.

  • Lü, Pin. 2027. “Weaponizing Intimacy: Emotional Proxy Repression against Feminist Activists in China.” Women’s Studies International Forum 120: 103440. Published version

    Theorizes emotional proxy repression, through which the state channels coercion via activists’ family members and converts bonds of obligation, care, and shame into instruments of pressure.

  • Lü, Pin. 2026. “VAWIP in a State-Monopolized Political Field: The Case of Chinese Feminist Activism.” Politics & Gender 22 (3): 879–885. Critical Perspectives symposium on Violence against Women Political Activists. Published version · Manuscript

    Identifies three conditions that make violence against women in politics especially effective in China: a state with strong coercive capacity, markets with limited autonomy from the state, and a gendered social order that disciplines women’s conduct.
